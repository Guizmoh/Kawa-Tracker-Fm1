The KAWA manual BETA

KAWA turns the M-VAVE FM-1 into a pocket tracker for composing in any style. You get six tracks, each with its own engine: pulse, wave, noise, FM, drum kit or sample. On top of that come patterns that you chain into a song, 32 effects to play live, and tools that write beats for you.

This manual gets straight to the point: install KAWA, then five tutorials to make a real song, then the details of the sound and the effects. Version 0.4 beta.

Beta version: read this first

KAWA 0.4 builds for the FM-1 and passes every check of its platform: size, memory, hardware access. It runs in the simulator and in the web emulator. However, it has not been tried on a real FM-1 yet.

Installing an unofficial firmware is at your own risk. The install path is the one used by Dinghy and Felucca, which already run on real FM-1s, and it has several safety nets (see below). Plug the FM-1 into a stable power supply and never unplug it during an install.

Projects cannot be moved to a computer yet. If something seems odd, say so: that is what a beta is for.

Composing with a tracker

KAWA is not made for one style. Pop, techno, hip hop, ambient, jazz, rock, video game music, chiptune, breakcore: all of it can be written in a tracker. What changes is the way you compose, which is very different from the usual music software (a DAW).

Music is written from top to bottom

Time runs down: each row is a step, a sixteenth note (four rows to a beat). Each column is a track. Each cell says what to play at that exact step: a note, its volume and an effect.

You read the song like a scrolling score, and you write it with the keys, note by note, at the exact spot. No mouse, no zoom: the grid is always the same.

In a DAWIn KAWA
TimeRuns left to right, on a timelineRuns down row by row; a row is a step
Writing a noteDraw it in a piano rollPlay it on the keys on the row you want (REC)

The songClips laid on a timelineShort patterns chained in the SONG, each played N times

What you gain: the whole song reads at a glance, to the step; your hands stay on the keys and knobs; you write a pattern quickly, vary it (CLONE, MAGIC) and chain it. The tutorials below walk the whole way, from an empty song to a finished one.

01Install KAWA

You need a USB data cable, a computer and Python 3 (python.org). The firmware is the file kawa-0.4.fwsc in the install/ folder.

  1. Close any other MIDI software, then plug in the FM-1, switched on.
  2. Easiest: double-click INSTALL-Windows.bat or INSTALL-Mac.command (Linux: ./INSTALL-Linux.sh). The first time it fetches what it needs (rtmidi2); it checks the package, asks you to confirm, sends the loader, then the application.
  3. By hand: pip3 install rtmidi2, then python3 install/fm1_install.py --info to check what the FM-1 runs, then python3 install/fm1_install.py install/kawa-0.4.fwsc.
  4. The FM-1 restarts into KAWA: the dog appears, then the demo.

What the install never writes

  • The start of the memory, with the chip's bootloader and its configuration.
  • The projects and samples area, from 0x97000 to 0xE0000, and the settings at 0xFC000.
  • An update keeps your projects and samples. The projects of 0.2 open in 0.4 as they were (they are converted when they load, and saved in the new format). Projects made with 0.1 are not read (their format changed); the demo starts instead, and they are replaced as soon as you save.

The safety nets

  • Nothing is committed before the end. If sending fails, the staging area is erased and the old firmware keeps running.
  • The whole package is checked before anything is erased. A damaged or incomplete package is refused. If a sector arrives different during the write, it is read again, never written.
  • Power cut during the install: the loader resumes at the next power-on. Run the installer again.
  • The USB rescue: hold OCT− while you switch on. KAWA then starts without sound, with only the update. If KAWA crashes twice in a row within 30 seconds of power-on, the FM-1 goes there by itself.
  • As a last resort: the chip's boot ROM still answers over USB (WL80UBOOT). FM-1 Transporter can then write the official firmware back, but it needs extra hardware, and the FM-1 reverse-engineering work considers this path not yet proven on retail FM-1s.

02Back to the official firmware

If you don't like KAWA, you can reinstall the official firmware with M-VAVE's tool. This is the most delicate step, because it rewrites the bootloader too: take your time.

  1. Your KAWA projects will be lost. The official firmware cannot read them.
  2. Plug the FM-1 into a stable power supply, with a USB data cable, and close any other MIDI software.
  3. If KAWA misbehaves, hold OCT− while you switch on: the screen shows “KAWA USB RESCUE”.
  4. Run M-VAVE's M-UPGRADE with the official firmware V15 (identity FM-1_015, SHA-256 db1642b2…7edb8a).
  5. Before the official tool writes anything, KAWA checks two things: that it really is M-VAVE's updater, and that the boot area of the official package is exactly the one already in your FM-1 (the official tool rewrites it, so it may only put back the same bytes). If either one does not match, nothing is written and KAWA keeps running.
  6. Unplug nothing during the transfer. If it stops halfway, plug back in and start it again.
  7. Check the result: python3 install/fm1_install.py --info should answer FM-1_015.

04First steps

  1. The tracks

    At power-on, the “KAWA QUEST” demo is loaded. Each column is a track, with its own colour. The track under the cursor is shown wide: the note, its volume as a small bar, and the cell's effect. The numbers on the left are the rows; each row lasts a sixteenth note.

  2. Play

    Press PLAY. The row that plays lights up, and the cells flash when a note starts. At the top right, you see the position in the chain, the tempo and the current beat. PLAY again stops.

  3. Help, everywhere

    Hold HOME on any page. KAWA then shows what the four knobs do on this page, the three encoders and the useful combinations. Let go of HOME to go back where you were.

05Tutorial 1 · A new song

  1. Open the projects menu

    Press SAVE once. At the top: the song's name, its slot in brackets, and a star if it has changed since the last save. SELECT moves through the menu, OCT+ confirms, OCT− goes back.

  2. New song

    Go up to NEW SONG and press OCT+. If the current song is not saved, KAWA asks you first.

  3. A brand-new song

    The song gets a random name, which you will change when you save it. Its six tracks already have a sound each: a pulse lead, the FM, a wave bass, noise hi-hats, a kit and a break.

  4. Choose a track's sound

    ALGORITHM picks the track, then EDIT opens its SOUND page. The strip at the top shows the six engines. PRESETS browses the factory sounds, and the four knobs shape the sound.

  5. Change the engine

    On this first page, SELECT changes the track's engine: PULSE, WAVE, NOISE, FM, KIT or SAMPLE. FM is greyed out if another track already has it: only one FM track at a time, so the FM-1 never runs out of breath.

  6. A factory sound

    Turn PRESETS to try the factory sounds. A star after the name means you have changed it. Play the keys to hear it.

06Tutorial 2 · Write a pattern

  1. Record notes

    Go back to the tracks with HOME, press REC (the cursor turns red), then play notes. Each note is written at the cursor's row, and the cursor moves down. When the scale is locked (SCALE page), the keys stay in the scale.

  2. The cursor step

    Hold REC and turn SELECT: you set how many rows the cursor jumps after each note (0 to 16). With 2, you write eighth notes; with 4, quarter notes. The value shows at the top left of the tracker.

  3. Edit a cell

    Place the cursor with SELECT. K1 changes the note, K2 its volume, K3 the cell's effect and K4 the effect's value (here a minor arpeggio). ARP clears the cell. Hold ARP and turn SELECT to insert or remove a row.

  4. Drums on the grid

    Pick the kit's track with ALGORITHM and press SEQ. The 16 white keys are the 16 steps, as on a Pocket Operator: press a key to place the sound, press it again to remove it. K1 picks the drum that will be placed. Hold a step to edit it with the knobs. The black keys change the track.

07Tutorial 3 · More patterns

  1. Magic

    LFO opens the 16 magic tools, one per white key. K1 sets their amount, K2 the die, K3 the scope: the track alone or the whole pattern.

  2. A beat in one key

    Key 2: BEAT writes a rhythm on the track. Press it again for another one. Every tool can be undone: SAVE held + OCT−.

  3. Clone the pattern

    Key 12: CLONE copies the pattern into the next empty pattern, adds it to the chain right after, and takes you there. It is the fastest way to make a variation.

  4. Change pattern

    You are now on P02, which you can change without touching P01. PRESETS moves from one pattern to another (32 patterns of 1 to 32 rows). During playback, if you change pattern, the screen stays on the one you are editing and shows “>P01” for the one that plays.

08Tutorial 4 · Chain patterns into a song

  1. The chain

    GLO opens SONG: the list of patterns in the order of the song. Each entry shows a pattern and how many times it plays. SELECT picks an entry, PRESETS changes its pattern.

  2. Repeat a pattern

    K3 sets how many times the entry plays its pattern: from ×1 to ×16. A four-bar verse made of two patterns? Set each one to ×2.

  3. Add entries

    Go to the “+” at the end and press a white key: key 1 adds pattern 1, key 2 pattern 2… OCT+ copies the entry under the cursor right after it, OCT− removes it.

  4. Loop or song

    ARP switches from LOOP (the pattern on the screen loops, handy for composing) to SONG (the whole song, entry after entry). K1 sets the tempo, K2 the swing and K4 the pattern's length.

  5. Listen to the song

    On the starting entry, press PLAY. The entry that plays lights up, and dots count its repeats. At the end of the chain, the song starts again from the beginning.

09Tutorial 5 · Save

  1. Save as

    SAVE, then SAVE or SAVE AS… and OCT+. Choose one of the three slots with SELECT (or a white key, 2 to 4). If the slot is already taken, KAWA asks before writing over it.

  2. Give it a name

    SELECT picks the letter to change, K1 changes it, K2 picks a random name, K3 clears it all. OCT+ saves.

  3. After that, in two presses

    The song keeps its slot: SAVE then OCT+ saves it again in the same place. LOAD… opens a song, RENAME… changes its name. On top of that, KAWA saves by itself into the AUTO slot when you are not playing.

Undo and redo

Hold SAVE: OCT− undoes the last change to a pattern, OCT+ redoes it. Releasing SAVE after holding it, with nothing else, undoes once. KAWA keeps the last six steps.

The pages of a sound

EDIT goes from one page to the next: SOUND and SOUND 2 (the engine's settings), for FM OPERATOR and OP ENVELOPE, for SAMPLE SAMPLE EDIT (below), then EFFECT, LFO, MODULATION and the track's TABLE (its own section). The small squares at the top right show where you are.

An effect on the synth or on the master? On a sound's EFFECT page, the effect only touches this track. For the whole song, go to MIX (ENV twice) and turn ALGORITHM past track 6: column M is the master, and its four knobs set its effect.

11Live effects

  1. Hold FX, play the keys

    Hold FX and press white keys: loops, stutters, scratch, reverse… Let go, and the sound comes back. A short press on FX keeps the page open. The wave at the top shows the sound, and the tile of an active effect has a bar that follows its cycle. On the other pages, a banner at the top tells you which effect is playing.

  2. Two banks, one target

    OCT+ switches to the WRECK bank: filters, freeze, distort, vinyl… K4 picks the target: the MASTER, or a single track (for example, loop only the break). The black keys mute tracks. With REC on and playback running, the effects you play are recorded in the thin LIVE lane on the right of the tracker.

13Cell effects

Each cell has two effects, and both play. The first is chosen with K3 and set with K4; a gold icon shows it in the tracker. Hold ARP and the same two knobs set the second: while ARP is held, the tracker shows the second column in green, with the first one's icon next to it. A delayed note can keep its pitch, a vibrato can have its own pan, a roll can be crushed.

  1. The second column

    Here, ARP held: the second effect of the cell under the cursor is a HOP to row 9, in green; the gold icon after it is its first effect. Let go of ARP and the tracker shows the first column again. A tap on ARP alone still clears the cell.

14Tables

A table is a small sequence played inside every note of a track, as on the M8. Its 16 steps come one after the other, every SPEED ticks (6 ticks make a row). Each step can move the pitch over the note, set the volume and play a trick. You write a single note in the tracker, and the table plays a chip arpeggio, a drum roll that grows, a kick that drops in pitch, a slice that stutters.

  1. EDIT until TABLE

    The table is the last page of a sound. Row 0 holds its settings: K1 the speed (in ticks, 6 = a row), K2 the length, K3 the step it loops back to after the last one (or none: the last step stays), K4 on or off. SELECT goes down the steps. On a step: K1 the pitch over the note (±24 semitones; on a KIT, the drum's tuning), K2 the volume (-- leaves it as it is), K3 the trick, K4 its value. ARP clears the step. The step playing lights up in the track's colour, and the bars at the bottom draw the pitch of each step.

TrickWhat it does when its step comes
HITPlays the note again (the drum, the slice, the synth), at its step's pitch
CUTStops the note
VIB, BNDVibrato, pitch bend from here (0: no more)
SHP, CRUThe waveform; crushed bits
VSL, PANThe volume slides from here (0: it stops); the note's pan
HOPGoes on to step N after this one

The table starts again with each note and keeps going while the note sounds; it stops with the song. A HIT only plays while the note is held: an OFF in the tracker ends it. Three tables to try:

  • A chip arpeggio: speed 2, length 3, loop to step 1; steps 0, +4, +7 (or +3 for minor).
  • A roll that grows: on a KIT, speed 3, length 4: four HITs, their volumes 6, 9, 12, 15, looping to step 4.
  • A falling kick: on a WAVE or PULSE, speed 1, steps 0, −2, −4, −6, −8… and no loop.

15The groove

  1. GLO twice

    The groove sets how long each row lasts, in ticks: 6 ticks is a straight sixteenth. Its steps play in turn, one per row: 7 then 5 is a light swing, 8 then 4 a heavy one, 4 4 4 a triplet feel. When it is on, it replaces the swing of the SONG page. K1 sets the ticks of the chosen step (SELECT or a white key chooses it), K2 the length, K3 a preset (it turns the groove on), K4 on or off.

    The strip under the bars shows one turn as it plays. When the ticks add up to 6 a row (7 + 5), the bar keeps its length; otherwise the count turns red, and the pattern gets shorter or longer. The MIDI clock out sends the ticks as they play: a drum machine follows the groove.

16Your own samples

KAWA has four slots for your sounds: MY 1 to MY 4, about 1.7 s each at 22 kHz. They show up as a SOURCE of the SAMPLE engine, next to the breaks.

  • In the emulator: drop a sound file on the page, or choose it in the “Your samples” panel. It is converted for you: mono, 22 kHz, normalised.
  • On the FM-1: python3 install/kawa_samples.py put 1 my-sound.wav, or the emulator's “Send to my FM-1” button. KAWA must be stopped while it receives: the sound cuts out for a moment during the write. If the transfer is cut off, the slot stays empty, never damaged.
  • Then, on the SOUND page of a SAMPLE track, set SOURCE to MY 1 to 4 and choose the MODE: SLICES on the white keys, or NOTES, played on the notes (C4 = the original pitch).

Edit a sample

  1. EDIT until SAMPLE EDIT

    On a SAMPLE track, a break or one of your sounds. Nothing is rewritten in the sound: the edit only says how it plays, and it is kept with the project, track by track. SELECT picks the marker: START, END, then the slices 2 to 16 (in SLICES mode). K1 moves it, K2 finely (its place shows in milliseconds), and you hear the slice. Move a slice and the others stay where they were; ARP spreads them evenly again. K3 NORM brings the loudest point of the edited part to full scale (up to ×16), K4 plays it backwards.

18Options

  1. Theme, language, clock, font

    GLO three times. K1 picks the theme (KAWA, GAME BOY, BONBON), K2 the language of the screen (English, French, Spanish), K4 the font of the screen: SMOOTH (the default), BASIC (KAWA's first), CLEAR (made for reading) or 8-BIT (arcade); each one is shown written in itself. K3 sets the MIDI clock over USB:

    • OUT: KAWA sends start, stop and the tempo (the groove's ticks too), to sync a drum machine or a program on your computer.
    • IN: KAWA follows another machine. Its START starts the song where PLAY would, its STOP stops it, its tempo leads (it shows in green, measured over four beats); each row waits for its ticks or catches up, and starts a few milliseconds after its tick. With IN and no clock, PLAY waits for one, and says so.

    The settings are kept when you switch off.

    The CPU line tells you how much of the processing time the sound uses. Above 70%, KAWA lightens the sound by itself, reverb first, rather than crackle.

19Help and troubleshooting

  • I forgot what a knob does: look at the bottom line, or hold HOME.
  • No sound: turn up MASTER; on MIX, check that no track is muted (a red M); check the note's volume with K2.
  • “FM: ONE TRACK ONLY”: another track already has FM; change its engine first.
  • A change went wrong: SAVE held + OCT−.
  • KAWA no longer starts: hold OCT− while you switch on (the USB rescue), then reinstall KAWA or the official firmware.
